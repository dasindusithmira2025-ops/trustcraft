## Slide 14 — Market evidence

### Claim 1 — "76.1% of Sri Lanka's craft and related trades workers are in the informal sector"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Figure** | 1,105,729 craft and related trades workers; 841,588 (76.1%) informal, 264,141 (23.9%) formal |
| **Organisation** | Department of Census and Statistics, Sri Lanka |
| **Title** | *Sri Lanka Labour Force Survey — Annual Report 2023* |
| **Location in source** | Chapter 7, **Table 7.5** — "Distribution of informal / formal sector employment by main occupation – 2023", occupation group 7 |
| **URL** | https://www.statistics.gov.lk/Resource/en/LabourForce/Annual_Reports/LFS2023.pdf |
| **Published** | 2024 |
| **Used on** | Slide 14 (giant metric, proportion rule, body copy) |

**Why this occupation group.** ISCO major group 7, "Craft and Related Trades
Workers", is the classification that contains plumbers, electricians,
carpenters, masons and related building trades — the exact supply side
TrustCraft addresses. It is used rather than a whole-economy figure because the
whole-economy figure would overstate our relevance.

**Supporting figures from the same report** (available if pressed, not on the slide):
- All-island informal sector share of total employment: **58.0%** (Table 7.1)
- Non-agricultural informal share: **47.1%** (Table 7.1)
- Colombo district informal share: **38.4%** (Table 6.4)
- Construction / electricity, gas, steam / water supply industry group: **77.3% informal** (Figure 7.2) — this is the backup line in the speaker script

### Claim 2 — The definition of "informal"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Claim** | A worker is classified informal when the organisation is unregistered (not in EPF or Inland Revenue), keeps no formal accounts, or has fewer than ten regular employees |
| **Organisation** | Department of Census and Statistics, Sri Lanka |
| **Title** | *Sri Lanka Labour Force Survey — Annual Report 2023*, Chapter 7 highlights, "Key aspects of identifying informal sector" |
| **URL** | https://www.statistics.gov.lk/Resource/en/LabourForce/Annual_Reports/LFS2023.pdf |
| **Used on** | Slide 14, right column, first fact |

This definition is quoted because it is the argument: the state that makes a
worker "informal" in the statistics is precisely the state that leaves a
customer with nothing to check.

## Slide 15 — Competitive position

Positions on the map are **qualitative judgements** made from each platform's
own public marketing site, and the slide says so in its footnote. No market
share, revenue or user figure is claimed for any competitor, because none is
public.

### Servixy

| | |
|---|---|
| **URL** | https://www.servixy.lk/ |
| **What we observed** | Category-based discovery across 10 home-service categories (plumbing, electrical, cleaning, painting, carpentry, AC & appliance repair, pest control, gardening, masonry & tiling, security & CCTV). Alternative flow: "Post a job" and receive quotes. Searchable provider directory filterable by skill, area and rate type. |
| **Trust mechanisms observed** | "NIC-Verified Providers"; providers "verified before they can accept jobs"; work history and verified customer reviews; star ratings |
| **Commercial model observed** | Posting a job and receiving quotes is free for customers; "no hidden fees" |
| **Not observed** | Any AI interpretation, photo input or voice input on the public site |

### TaskForce.lk

| | |
|---|---|
| **URL** | https://taskforce.lk/ |
| **What we observed** | Three-step flow stated on the site: "Post Your Job — describe what you need, **set your budget**"; "Receive Offers — verified, NIC-checked contractors send competitive bids"; "Get It Done — pick the best offer, pay securely into escrow, release on completion". Categories span home trades *and* creative/digital services. |
| **Trust mechanisms observed** | NIC checks and background verification before bidding; payment held in escrow, released on customer confirmation; team-mediated dispute resolution |
| **Direct quote used in prep** | "Trust isn't a feature. It's the whole point." |
| **Not observed** | Any AI interpretation, photo input or voice input on the public site |

### Blu

| | |
|---|---|
| **URL** | https://www.blu.lk/ |
| **What we observed** | Category browsing (cleaning, electrical, plumbing, AC service, carpentry, "view all"). Colombo-focused with stated expansion intent. |
| **Trust mechanisms observed** | "Professional profiles are reviewed before they start serving customers"; booking records; customer feedback; local human support |
| **Direct quote used in prep** | "Trust should not depend on luck or a forwarded phone number." |
| **Not observed** | Any AI interpretation, photo input or voice input on the public site |

### Also reviewed, not plotted individually

Findit.lk, ProFIT (profit.lk), HandyMaster (handymaster.lk), SourceTradesman,
OnaWadak (onawadak.lk) and HodaBass (hodabass.lk) were reviewed during
research. All are category-first directory or booking products. They are
represented on the map by the muted "Directory listings" point rather than
named, because naming nine competitors would make the slide unreadable and we
did not review each deeply enough to place it individually.

Noted during research: QuickHelp (quickhelp.lk) announced it was pausing B2C
operations from 1 July 2025. This is **not** used in the deck — it is a single
site notice and we did not corroborate it.

### The axes

- **X — "Browse a category" → "Start from the problem".** Assessed on what the
  product asks for first. Every named competitor asks for a category, or for a
  job description plus a budget. This is directly observable on their sites.
- **Y — "A list of names" → "Evidence you can act on".** Assessed on what is
  shown at the moment of choosing. Verification badges and star averages place
  a product mid-axis; a composite score with its drivers exposed, plus an
  explained match ranking, places it higher. This axis is a **judgement**, and
  the slide labels the axes qualitative for that reason.

**Fairness note carried into the speaker script:** Servixy and TaskForce both
verify against NIC and both hold escrow; TaskForce also mediates disputes.
These are genuine strengths and the presenter says so out loud. The claimed
difference is the entry point, not the safeguards.

this repository rather than in a publication.

## Deliberately absent

The deck makes **no** claim about any of the following, because we could not
source them honestly:

- Market size in currency, TAM/SAM/SOM
- Any competitor's revenue, users, provider count or market share
- TrustCraft users, revenue, providers, transactions, partnerships or pilots
- Growth rates, projections, valuations or launch dates
- Customer acquisition cost, average job value, or willingness to pay
- Any user-research finding, interview or survey — we ran none
- Any pricing for the proposed provider tier

If a judge asks for one of these, the answer is in `TrustCraft_Judge_QA.md`
under "Questions that deserve 'I don't know'".
